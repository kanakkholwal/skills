# Outreach Writer: Full Guide

A portable playbook for writing cold outreach and job-application messages that get replies. Persona-agnostic: works for anyone reaching out to a founder, recruiter, or hiring team. Fill in the sender's own proof and the target's real details.

Grounded in one core idea: the reader is busy and gets hundreds of these. **Make it stupidly easy to say yes.** Evidence of execution beats credentials.

---

## The four things that actually get replies

1. **Proof of execution**: real things shipped, with links and numbers, not theoretical skill.
2. **Agency**: solving problems independently, without hand-holding.
3. **Ownership**: treating the work as your own, seeing it through.
4. **Relevance**: why THIS target, and how the sender helps THEM specifically.

---

## Process, in order

### 1. Research the target first (mandatory)

Never write from a job description alone. Before drafting, find:
- What they actually build (fetch site, GitHub, product, docs).
- Their stack and architecture (so proof can be matched to it).
- Their stage and size (seed vs growth changes the pitch).
- Who runs it and how technical they are (tunes how much tech to include).

The message must reference something specific and true about them. Naming a real product decision, feature, or architecture choice is the single biggest signal that this isn't a mass blast.

### 2. Select proof

Pick the **three strongest** shipped things that map to what the target does. Prefer:
- Real users and numbers over titles or coursework.
- Things with a verifiable link (live URL or repo).
- Work structurally similar to the target's problem.

### 3. Draft, then cut

Write to the structure below, then remove every line that isn't context, proof, or the ask.

---

## Structure

### Cold DM (Twitter / LinkedIn)

Shortest possible. Shape:
```
[One line of context: who you are + why them, specifically]
[Proof point 1: built X, outcome/number, matched to their problem]
[Proof point 2: built Y, outcome/number]
[Proof point 3: built Z, outcome/number]
[Portfolio / GitHub link]
[One simple ask: a 15-min call]
```
Skimmable in ~15 seconds.

### Email

Can run slightly longer, still under 90 seconds to read:
- **Background:** 1-2 lines max.
- **Why this company specifically:** reference their real product/architecture; show you looked.
- **Why you fit:** work samples with links, "I built X, used by Y."
- **Links** (portfolio, GitHub, LinkedIn), resume attached if applying.
- **One clear ask.**

Use bullets over prose. No paragraph longer than 2-3 lines.

---

## Rules

- **Value to them, not admiration of them.** "Here's how I help you ship/grow" beats "I really like what you're building."
- **First two lines carry the point.** No warmup, no life story.
- **Specific, not vague.** "Built a schema-driven tool builder that replaced 20 hardcoded apps" beats "worked on backend systems."
- **Numbers earn trust.** Users, growth %, latency cut, revenue, scale.
- **Why them, not any founder.** One concrete, genuine reason tied to their work.
- **Projects prove skills.** Don't write "strong at X"; show the thing that required X.
- **One ask.** A short call. Don't stack multiple requests.
- **Multi-channel is fine.** Email + DM doubles odds; reference that you did both.

---

## Never do this

- Em-dashes.
- AI-tell phrases: "thrilled", "passionate", "leverage", "excited to", "delve", "I've been following your work", "love your trajectory", "resonate".
- Fake compliments or buttering up.
- Generic passion statements ("I'm passionate about AI").
- Reaching, cheap similarity hooks ("we have so much in common").
- Tech-stack dumps: listing ten technologies reads junior. Tune tech to the reader.
- Claims without a link, or invented/inflated numbers (instant credibility kill).
- Screenshots as proof; links do that.
- "Applied" when still applying.
- Desperation, over-apologizing, or "sorry to bother you."

---

## Tone

Plain, direct, human, confident without bluffing. A builder talking to a builder, not a candidate performing for an employer. Short declarative sentences. If a line sounds like a cover letter, cut it.

---

## Honesty guardrails

- If there's a gap (years of experience, timezone, a missing category), state it once, plainly, and move on. Don't hide it, don't dwell.
- Never claim a tool, program, or employer category the sender doesn't genuinely have. Reference checks and two follow-up questions expose bluffs, which is far worse than the gap.
- If AI assisted the work, don't disclaim it unprompted and don't deny it if asked. Frame as "designed / architected / drove" for the parts that were genuinely the sender's judgment.

---

## Follow-ups

- Follow up **once**, then stop. A second chase reads as desperate.
- Follow up only if the first message was genuinely strong; follow-ups amplify existing signal, they don't create it.
- Give a real reason to re-surface (a new shipped thing, a relevant post), not just "any update?"

---

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Did I research the target and name something specific and true about them?
- [ ] Do the first two lines get to the point?
- [ ] Three proof points, each with an outcome/number and a link?
- [ ] Is proof matched to what the target actually does?
- [ ] Value to them, not admiration?
- [ ] One clear ask (a short call)?
- [ ] Portfolio / GitHub included?
- [ ] No em-dashes, no AI-tell phrases, no fake compliments?
- [ ] No invented numbers, every claim linked?
- [ ] Under 90 seconds to read (DM: ~15 seconds)?

## Worked templates (fill in the brackets)

### Cold DM

```
Hi [name], [one line: who you are + the specific reason it's them].
Closest thing I've built to what you do: [specific build], [number/outcome]. [One line mapping it to their problem.]
Also built [build 2, number] and [build 3, number].
[portfolio/github link]
Open to a 15-minute call?
```

### Application email

```
Subject: [Role], [Your Name]

Hi [name/team],

Applying for the [role]. [1 line: location/remote + one-line identity.]

[Why them + your closest-matching build, 2-3 lines, with the outcome.]

Also built:
- [Build 1] ([link]): [what it is], [number].
- [Build 2] ([link]): [what it is], [number].
- [Build 3] ([link]): [what it is], [number].

[Optional: one honest line on a gap.]

GitHub: [link]
Portfolio: [link]
Resume attached.

[Name]
```
